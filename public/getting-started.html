Myth: Airing out the house is enough to get rid of smoke smell.

Fact: While ventilation helps a bit, smoke particles are microscopic and get embedded into porous surfaces like drywall, carpets, and upholstery. Just opening windows won't remove these particles, leading to persistent odors and potential health issues. Professional cleaning and deodorization are usually necessary.

Myth: You can clean smoke-damaged items with household cleaners.

Fact: Many common household cleaners can actually set smoke stains or spread soot, making the damage worse and harder to remove. Corrosive ash needs specific, specialized cleaning agents and techniques to be safely and effectively removed without causing further damage.

Myth: Smoke damage isn't as serious as fire damage.

Fact: Smoke can cause extensive damage far beyond what's immediately visible. It can corrode metals, stain surfaces permanently, and leave behind toxic residues that pose health risks. Structural integrity can also be compromised by extreme heat, even without direct flames. Understanding the full extent of damage requires an expert eye.

Myth: You don't need to worry about smoke damage if there's no visible soot.

Fact: Smoke can travel through HVAC systems and vents, permeating hidden areas like wall cavities and attics. Even if you don't see soot, smoke odors indicate particle presence, which means potential contamination. A thorough inspection by a certified technician is crucial to identify and address all hidden damage.
