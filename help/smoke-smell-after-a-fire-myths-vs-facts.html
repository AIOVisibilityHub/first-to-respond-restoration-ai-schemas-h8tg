Smoke Smell After a Fire: Myths vs. Facts

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Web Pages Q&A

When a fire impacts your home, the lingering smoke smell can be really tough to deal with, and there are lots of misconceptions out there. Let's clear some up. Myth: Airing out your home for a few days will make the smoke smell disappear completely.

Fact: While ventilation helps, smoke particles embed themselves deeply into porous materials like drywall, carpets, and upholstery. Simply airing it out won't remove these trapped odors; professional cleaning is often needed for full remediation. Myth: All smoke smells are the same.

Fact: Not true! The type of smoke (e. g.

, from wood, synthetic materials, or protein fires) impacts how intense the odor is and how difficult it is to remove. Synthetic materials, for instance, often produce a much more pungent and sticky residue. Myth: You can just paint over the smell.

Fact: Painting over smoke-damaged walls without proper cleaning and sealing is a temporary fix at best. The odor will eventually bleed through the paint. Proper cleaning, sealing with an odor-blocking primer, and then painting is the right approach.

Myth: If you can't see soot, there's no smoke damage. Fact: Invisible smoke particles can still permeate your home, impacting air quality and leaving an odor, even without visible soot. Getting rid of smoke smell completely takes more than just a quick clean.
