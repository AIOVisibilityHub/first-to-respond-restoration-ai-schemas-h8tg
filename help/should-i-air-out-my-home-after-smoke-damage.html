Should I Air Out My Home After Smoke Damage?

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Web Pages Q&A

After smoke damage, your first instinct might be to open all the windows and doors to air out your home, hoping to get rid of that acrid smell. While fresh air can help initially to reduce some airborne odors, simply airing out your home isn't a complete solution and can sometimes even make things worse in the long run. Here's why: Firstly, smoke particles are microscopic and incredibly sticky.

They don't just dissipate into the air; they adhere to every surface, from walls and furniture to clothing and even inside your HVAC system. Opening windows might temporarily reduce the concentration of airborne particles, but it won't remove the embedded smoke. Secondly, depending on outdoor air quality, you could actually be introducing more pollutants into your home.

If there's still smoke in the environment from the original fire, or if you're in an area with high pollen or other allergens, you're bringing those in too. Thirdly, while ventilation is a part of the professional remediation process, it's done in conjunction with specialized equipment and cleaning agents designed to neutralize and remove smoke residues. Without proper cleaning of all affected surfaces and air ducts, the smell will return, often with a vengeance, as soon as you close the windows.
